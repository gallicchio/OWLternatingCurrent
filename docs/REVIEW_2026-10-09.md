# Design review, round 1 (2026-10-09)

Five reviews of rev A before the order: Claude's own (part A, written before reading the
others), then four other LLMs' (`reviews/review_GPT/`, `reviews/review_Gemini/`,
`reviews/review_Grok/`, `reviews/review_KimiK3/`; part B says what each found, what was
taken and why, and ranks them), and the changes that came out of it all (part C).
Severity as in `DESIGN_REVIEW.md` section 0: blocker, major, minor, nit.

## A. Claude's review (independent, before reading the four)

Read: `gen/sch_base.py`, `gen/sch_boards.py`, `gen/parts.py`, `gen/pins.py`,
`gen/connectors.py`, `gen/symbols.py`, the placement and panel generators, the firmware's
coil driver and constants, the release files, and the datasheets of every active part
(pinouts, straps, ratings).

### A.1 Findings

**A1 (major): the perch bridges may not start.**  The UCC27524 (U9, U10) runs from the
4.8 V rail, and its ground pin sits on the 0.5 ohm sense resistor (R0R5), so the chip sees
VDD minus the shunt's drop.  Its under-voltage lockout starts at 4.2 V typical but up to
4.5 V at 25 C and 4.65 V over temperature (datasheet, UVLO table).  Worst case the rail is
4.8 V - 2.5 % (the TPS54302's reference, 0.581-0.611 V) - 1.4 % (1 % resistors) = 4.61 V,
and at the 0.24 A the sim expects the shunt drops 0.12 V: the chip sees 4.49 V, at or
under its worst-case start threshold.  Typical units start (4.78 V against 4.2 V), a
worst-case one does not.  **Fix:** shunt 0.5 -> 0.1 ohm (C25334, Basic, 1206) so the
drop is 24 mV, and the sense amplifier's gain 20.6 -> 101 (R5K1 -> R1K in the 100 k
feedback) so the firmware still sees 10.1 V/A (`FAM_ISENSE_V_PER_A`).  Margin becomes
90 mV against the 25 C worst case.  Raising the rail instead would push the NS4150B past
its recommended 5.0 V (its absolute maximum is 5.25 V), which is why the rail is 4.8 V.

**A2 (major): the theremin's antennas are bare gold a child is told to touch, wired
straight to a 74LVC1G14's input.**  The input has 2 kV HBM protection; a child off a
carpet delivers 8-15 kV.  One discharge can kill the pitch or volume oscillator.  **Fix:**
an ESD diode at each antenna pad (ESD9L5.0ST5G, 0.9 pF so the oscillator hardly notices,
SOD-923) and 1 k in series between the pad and the oscillator's node, so the diode clamps
and the resistor isolates.

**A3 (major, manufacturing): the tree's two right-angle headers (J101, J102) and the
perch's socket (J201) are through-hole parts on the bottom side, and J102's pins reach
within 1.4 mm of the panel's outer edge** (the tree is turned with its top, J102's edge,
2.4 mm from the 5 mm rail, and the pins project 6 mm past the edge; J101's, at the foot,
reach 3.6 mm over the perch).  The base's through-hole parts (jack, socket, speaker) are
on top.  JLCPCB's conveyors hold the rails, and their through-hole process on the second
side is at best a query.  **Fix:** take J101, J102 and J201 out of JLCPCB's assembly (48
joints, ten minutes with an iron) and buy them loose; keep the rest assembled.  (Row 2 of
the panel has 30 mm of slack, so the tree could also move in by 8 mm; not done: with the
headers absent during assembly there is nothing to clear, and a wider gap would cost the
tree its frame tabs on that side, since a tab spans 6 mm at most.)

**A4 (minor): documentation that disagrees with the design.**  `gen/sch_base.py`'s I2C
note says the tree's LP5036 is at 0x31; it is at 0x32 (ADDR1 high).  `DESIGN_REVIEW.md`
says the buck has two 10 uF outputs; the schematic has two 22 uF.  `gen/parts.py`'s
`C68N_C0G` note calls it the bat's tank; the bat uses 100 nF (82 kHz) and the 68 nF is
unused.

**A5 (minor): the pendulum's Hall sensor is the coarse range.**  The TMAG5273A2's range
is +-133 mT; the 10 x 2 mm magnet 12 mm above it makes about 13 mT, a tenth of the
range.  The A1 (+-40 mT) would resolve three times finer.  Not changed: the 16-bit
reading has thousands of counts of signal either way, and the firmware only wants the
zero crossings.

**A6 (minor): the base's LP5036 can dissipate 1.2 W** if all 31 channels sit at 22 mA
with the anodes on 4.8 V (violet 3528 LEDs drop 3.2 V, orange 2.1 V), 43 C above
ambient at its 35.7 C/W.  Fine in itself; the firmware's scenes keep the average well
under that, and the chip has thermal shutdown.  Noted so the light show is not pushed to
full white.

**A7 (nit): `DESIGN_REVIEW.md` section 0 said three reviewers; four reviewed.**

### A.2 Section 10's questions (Claude's answers)

1. Power entry: Q1's drain is on the jack side, source on the load side, R1 pulls the gate
   down, D1's cathode is on the source: Vgs is clamped at -10 V.  Right.  The PTC and the
   SMBJ15A clamp (24.4 V at 24 A) are under every part's limit (TPS54302 30 V, DRV8874
   37 V, AO4407A 30 V).  12 V to the DRV8874s at 1 mm / 0.4 mm into the pins: 3.3 A for
   milliseconds is nothing for 1 oz copper; the 470 uF carries the pulses.
2. The buck: 2 x 22 uF (about 28 uF at 4.8 V bias) against the datasheet's 30 uF
   example; fine.  EN at 2.2 V from 12 V, 4.4 V at the clamp, under its 7 V.
3. Levitation: not re-derived here; the sim is the evidence (65 % by the designer's own
   estimate).
4. Familiars: see A1.  The token's LEDs at 150 ohm each: about 20 mA at the 4.8 V
   drive, under the 30 mA rating.
5. Theremin drift: a 74LVC1G14's thresholds move about 1 % over 20 C; the firmware
   tracks a slow baseline.  ESD is the real risk (A2).
6. GPIO binding: not re-run.
7. The panel: see A3.
8. CPL rotations: the predictions are consistent with JLCPCB's drawings; only their
   preview settles it.
9. The RP2354A's corner: decoupling 1.5-3 mm from the pins is a few nH; harmless at
   12 MHz.  XIN/XOUT are short (under 5 mm) with the 1 k on XOUT.
10. Child safety: magnets sealed in PLA, 12 V, 1 W speaker, mouse-bite edges to be
    sanded: fine.

### A.3 Checked and found right

TPS54302 pins and 4.80 V feedback; XC6206 pins; AO4407A pins (SSSGDDDD) and the zener's
sense; SMBJ15A and B5819W senses; NS4150B pins, its 240 k / 100 k = 2.4 gain, CTRL held low
by the switch; 74HC4051 pins (A0-A7, S0-S2, E, VEE); 74LVC1G14 pins; LSM6DS3TR-C (SA0
low = 0x6A, CS high for I2C, SDx/SCx to ground as the datasheet allows); LTR-303 pins;
TMAG5273 pins (pin 3 to ground as required) and 0x35; DRV5055 pins and 7.5 mV/mT at 3.3 V;
DRV8874 straps (PMODE high = PWM, IMODE ground = fixed off-time, automatic retry, nFAULT
on overcurrent only), ITRIP = 3.3 V / (450 uA/A x 2.2 k) = 3.33 A, VREF on 3.3 V, charge
pump 22 nF and 100 nF; UCC27524 inputs and the enables' 4.7 k pull-down; OPA2333 as a
non-inverting x20.6 within its rail-to-rail inputs; LP5036 IREF (105 x 0.7 V / 3.3 k =
22 mA), addresses 0x30 and 0x32, exposed pad on ground (pads 46, 47 in the netlist);
RP2354A supplies (VREG_AVDD through 33 ohm, VREG_VIN, LX through 3.3 uH, FB on the 1.1 V
node, DVDD x3, IOVDD, QSPI_IOVDD, USB_OTP_VDD, ADC_AVDD each with 100 nF), RUN open (it
has an internal pull-up: datasheet pin table), BOOTSEL = QSPI_SS low through 1 k, the
other QSPI pins open (the flash is in the package), 27 ohm USB series resistors, the
12 MHz crystal with 15 pF and 1 k; USB-C pads A4/A9/B4/B9 on VBUS and A1/A12/B1/B12 on
ground (stacked pins), CC1/CC2 5.1 k; all five I2C addresses distinct; the firmware's
bridge PWM (forward / brake / reverse, slow decay); the tanks (12.5 uH + 100 nF =
142 kHz, 12.5 uH + 22 nF = 303 kHz, 37 uH + 100 nF = 82 kHz); the two joints' pin tables
agree on every even pin; the 4.8 V budget (1.1 A LEDs + 0.5 A bridges + 0.3 A audio of
3 A); the 12 V budget; DRC, ERC and parity clean; the release's own checks.

### A.4 Could not check

JLCPCB's current rule on through-hole parts on the second side and on parts projecting

## B. The four reviews

Each finding was checked against the datasheets, the generators or a calculation before
it was taken.  "Taken" means it changed the design, the firmware, a simulation or a
document (part C says where).

### B.1 Kimi K3 (`reviews/review_KimiK3/`)

No `REPORT.md`, no verdict, no answer to six of the ten questions: four sub-agents each
left a `notes_*.md` (manufacturing, levitation, familiars, GPIO) and twenty scripts, and
nobody collated them.  Inside the notes is the deepest work of the four: it re-derived
every number, ran the simulations and the firmware's host tests in its copy, enumerated
the GPIO search space, and wrote a switching-level simulation of the DRV8874.

- **Taken, major:** the panel's clean-edge check was one-sided (`gen/panel.py`): a tab
  found from the tree's side landed its mouse bites inside the perch's owl notch, a
  seating surface the owl's wing hooks into.  Verified in the board file.  Fixed: the
  far board's clean edges are checked too.  That showed the perch had hung from its two
  notches: with both notch tabs gone it had one tab left (its other sides face the
  tree's canopy, the bat's wing and a 22 mm gap to the rail).  The tab search now
  tries a 30-40 mm side's fifths as well as its middle, which gives the perch two tabs
  to the base, either side of its thread hole, and a board with fewer than two tabs
  fails the build.
- **Taken, major:** the fiducials were on the top side only, on a panel assembled on
  both sides.  Fixed: the fiducial footprint now has a pad on each side.
- **Taken, major:** the levitation tables (`lev_table.h`, `lev_plant.h`) were computed
  for an earlier four-layer, 11-turn coil, 6 % stronger than the three-layer coil as
  drawn, and the generator no longer ran (its layer-count assertion).  Fixed: the
  generator models the coil on the stack-up's first three layers at the tracks'
  centre radii; regenerated (grip 18.9 mN/A, coil field at the sensor 2.89 mT/A); the
  host tests pass.
- **Taken, major:** at 40 kHz the levitation PWM ripples 2.5 A peak to peak on the
  30 uH coil, so the DRV8874's 3.3 A chopper cuts in above about 2 A of mean current and
  the delivered mean falls as the command rises (its table: 2.4 A asked, 1.6 A got).
  Grok found the same by hand.  Kimi's fix, 150-200 kHz, is past the DRV8874's 100 kHz
  specification; taken at 100 kHz (ripple 1 A) with `LEV_I_MAX_A` 3.0 -> 2.6 A so the
  peak stays under the chopper.
- **Taken, minor:** a DRV5055 at its -8 mT quiescent-offset corner never catches a bat
  resting 3 mm low with a 5 mm catch window (reproduced in the test harness): the window
  is 7 mm now.
- **Taken, minor:** the familiar firmware's `FAM_L1_H` was the sim's idealised 28.5 uH
  (drawn: 30.3); the sim's resistor sweep never included the built 150 ohm legs
  (GPT noticed this too); the familiar test's token legs were 68 ohm; the board's
  10.1 V/A sense case was reported, not asserted.  All four fixed; the test asserts it.
- **Taken, nit:** the per-coil resistance for the levitation coil (2.56, not the
  pendulum coils' 2.29 ohm); the perch PWM's reliance on the inverted B channel, now a
  comment; "drop in supply current" is a rise (two documents); 3.7 g is 3.6 g; stale
  prose in the SDK board header; `X_ISENSE` unused.
- **Taken, GPIO:** the search scored a fixed-cut proxy, not the cyclic count it reports;
  Kimi showed a binding with 76 true crossings where the search kept 84.  Fixed in
  `gen/assign.py` (a pool of the best candidates by the proxy, re-ranked exactly) and
  verified in a copy: 76.  Not applied to rev A's board, which routes clean as it is.
  Its other GPIO point, PWM on GPIO16-25 (48-63 crossings), is now a documented choice
  in `gen/pins.py`: the levitation's two ADC lines live on that edge.
- **Noted, not done:** the UCC27524's output resistance (ROH 5 ohm at 12 V, more at
  4.8 V) is in no model of the familiar loop: a bring-up measurement (`PLAN.md` 10);
  a resonant foreign object would be driven forever at 0.25 W (harmless); the
  levitation host test's plant and controller share one generator run; the two-magnet
  `levitation_loop.py` is now marked superseded; the bat hangs by two tabs.
- **Its UVLO finding** (minor, "low that any real unit fails") missed that the chip's
  ground rides the shunt: with the drop included the margin was gone (A1).
- Its cost check found the 27 ohm resistor's 6941-piece minimum came from JLCPCB's data,
  not the script: the part is swapped (Yageo, 1 %).

### B.2 Gemini (`reviews/review_Gemini/`)

A complete, well-ordered report with three patches, every question answered, good
arithmetic throughout (the buck's crossover, the TVS clamp, the levitation's fields).  It
over-calls severity (two "blockers" that are majors) and two of its eleven findings do
not hold.

- **Taken, major:** ESD on the antennas (the same as A2); its solder-mask alternative
  was not taken, the gold is the point.  Its series-resistor value (10-47 k) would
  decouple the antenna's capacitance at 500 kHz; 1 k with the diode is enough.
- **Taken, major:** the PTC.  Its 16 V argument is weak (a PTC's voltage rating is for
  the tripped state, where 12 V is what it sees), but its trip argument is right: a 3-5 A
  brick current-limits at about 6 A and could never trip a 10 A part, so a 4.5 A fault
  would cook at 54 W with the fuse holding.  F1 is now 3 A hold / 6 A trip, 24 V.
- **Taken, major:** the IMU's Z axis is inverted by its bottom-side placement.  True,
  and incomplete: KiCad mirrors a back-side footprint top to bottom (pad 1 at local
  (-1.26, -0.75) lands at (-1.26, +0.75) on the board), so Y flips too.  Both set, X
  kept, still to be confirmed by tilting the first board.
- **Taken, minor:** the amplifier's full scale (1.4 W into the 1 W speaker, over a toy's
  85 dB) depended on a firmware ceiling: the input resistors are 220 k now (gain 1.1,
  0.2 W).  Also: the mouse-bite edges must be sanded (said in `ORDER_LIST.md` now), the
  0x31/0x32 note (its patch 3; also A4).
- **Tried and reverted, minor:** the TMAG5273's 100 nF is 21 mm away because of the
  coil keep-outs.  A build with it 2.6 mm from the sensor, in the channel between the
  two rear coils, overlapped both coils' courtyards (an 0603's courtyard is 2.8 mm long)
  and blocked the one 0.6 mm pass through which the bridges' shared fault line threads
  the coils: the router could no longer close that net.  Back at 21 mm, with a note in
  `gen/placement.py`.  The sensor draws 2.5 mA at I2C speeds; the capacitor there is a
  reservoir, which is what it has.
- **Not taken:** its panel patch (move the tree 6 mm and the perch and bat 14 mm):
  the geometry is right, but the headers are no longer assembled (A3), and gaps wider
  than 6 mm would lose the tree its frame tabs.  The theremin's warm-up drift against
  the 0.3 % volume gate: plausible, but the firmware's baseline tracker already walks
  slow drifts (Grok read the code), and it is a one-constant tune on the bench if it
  shows.  C2 at 25 V against a 24.4 V clamp: a transient of microseconds on a ceramic
  rated for continuous 25 V; left.  A printed levitation cradle: wanted, not before the
  order (`PLAN.md` 10).
- Its section-10 answers are the best written of the four (the levitation's numbers
  reproduce the tables; the GPIO constraints are checked pin by pin).

### B.3 Grok (`reviews/review_Grok/`)

The best judgment of the four and the most honest about its limits ("I would not pay
until that is settled"; "I am not reporting a rotation I proved wrong").  No patches,
but every fix it asked for is specified exactly.

- **Taken, blocker for use:** the bat's snap-off cap holds a hazardous magnet and fits
  the small-parts cylinder: its flux index is ten times the toy limit, the cap is about
  25 x 13 x 6 mm, and `print/check.py` applies none of the use-and-abuse loads.  The
  magnets stay sealed in the prints; the cap is now glued to the bat once fitted
  (`print/README.md`, `ORDER_LIST.md`, `PLAN.md` 7).
- **Taken, major:** the headers off the assembly (A3), with the cleanest statement of
  why; the alternative it offers (sacrificial FR4 ears) is the other way to do it.
- **Taken, major:** the levitation's published loop result is the old plant and never
  included the DRV8874; at 12 V the coil reaches ITRIP in 8.3 us, inside one on-time
  at 2 A.  The same point as Kimi's switching sim, reached by hand; the PWM is 100 kHz
  now and the firmware's current cap 2.6 A.  Its first-article experiments (a current
  step with the Hall recorded; catch from 1 mm before 3 mm) are in `PLAN.md` 10.
- **Taken, nit:** three stale comments (the 10 uF, "pumpkin antenna", the familiar's
  coil constants).
- **Not taken:** the TPS54302's 108 % overvoltage comparator reaching 5.39 V at the
  amplifier (a transient in a stacked tolerance, as it says itself; left for a scope on
  the first board, as it says).
- Its section-10 answers are careful and mostly right; its 12 V copper arithmetic
  (IPC-2221) and its reading of the familiar current cap (6.1 V over 27 ohm) match
  mine.

## C. What changed

**Schematic and parts** (`gen/sch_base.py`, `gen/parts.py`):
- perch bridges: shunt 0.5 -> 0.1 ohm (C25334), amplifier gain x20.6 -> x101 (1 k),
  10.1 V/A (A1);
- theremin: ESD9L5.0ST5G (C82326, SOD-923) at each antenna pad, 1 k in series to the
  gate (A2, Gemini 2);
- audio: input resistors 100 k -> 220 k, gain 2.4 -> 1.1, full scale 0.2 W (Gemini 6);
- F1: BSMD2920-300-24V, 3 A hold / 6 A trip, 24 V (Gemini 3);
- the 27 ohm 0402: Yageo RC0402FR-0727RL (C138021), no 6941-piece minimum (Kimi);
- notes: 0x32, the fuse, the familiars' sense, the antennas (A4, Gemini 10, Grok 5).

**Placement and panel** (`gen/placement.py`, `gen/panel.py`, `gen/library.py`,
`gen/board.py`):
- the ESD diode and the 1 k beside each oscillator, on its side away from the shape
  (inside the shape's keep-out no via may go, so a diode at the near side could not be
  reached from the pad: the first rebuild showed it);
- the TMAG5273's capacitor tried beside the sensor and put back (Gemini 7, above);
- the far board's clean edges checked for every tab, a 30-40 mm side's fifths tried,
  fewer than two tabs on a board fails the build (Kimi mfg 1-2);
- fiducials on both sides (Kimi mfg 4).

**Release** (`mfg/make_release.py`): J101, J102, J201 in `HAND_SOLDER`: out of the BOM
and the CPL, into `hand_solder_bom.csv` with their codes; the PCB remark says
through-hole parts on the top side only (A3, Grok 2, Kimi mfg 3, Gemini 1).

**Firmware** (`firmware/`): `LEV_PWM_HZ` 40 -> 100 kHz, `LEV_I_MAX_A` 3.0 -> 2.6 A,
`FAM_ISENSE_V_PER_A` 10.1, `FAM_L1_H` 30.3 uH, `IMU_AXIS_Y/Z` -1, the catch window 7 mm,
the levitation coil's own resistance in the thermal model, the regenerated levitation
tables (`gen_lev_tables.py` on the three-layer coil), the familiar test's 150 ohm legs and
its asserted board-gain case, comments.  `make -C firmware/test`: all pass.

**Simulations** (`sim/physics/`): the resistor sweep with 150 ohm and the reachable
currents (at the most the bridge can push, 0.24 A at 142 kHz, the owl's LEDs take
9.7 / 7.0 mA; at the cat's 0.11 A, 12.4 / 9.5 mA); the stale "12 V bridge" print; the
README marks `levitation_loop.py` superseded.

**GPIO** (`gen/assign.py`): the exact cyclic count re-ranks the search's best
candidates; rev A keeps its binding (the board is routed and clean); rev B gets 76
crossings for free.

**Routing order** (`gen/routes.py`): with the new parts in the netlist the bridges'
shared fault line twice ended in three pieces, walled in by the LED channels, and the
rip-up could not free it; the two shared lines (sleep, fault) now go before the
RP2354A's other lines.

**Documents:** `README.md`, `ORDER_LIST.md` (the three connectors, glue, sanding, the
magnet hazard), `print/README.md` (glue the cap), `DESIGN_REVIEW.md`, `PLAN.md` (section
7 and a new section 10: the bench, the cradle, the loop port, rev B).

**Build after the changes** (`python3 build.py`, three passes of the board step: the
first found the sensor's capacitor and the diode's placement, the second the fault

violations, 0 unconnected, 0 parity at every severity; the panel 22 tabs (base 16,
