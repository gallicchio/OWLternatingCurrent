# Design review — Grok

Reviewer: Grok. Read the generators, the firmware, the physics results already in the tree, and the datasheets in place. Did not rebuild, did not run `build.py`, and did not copy datasheets or the Rosenberg checklist into this directory. Numbers I computed are in [calculations.py](calculations.py).

## 1. Verdict

Order after the named fixes below, not as a blind "send the zip." Confidence about 7/10 that the light show, the 12 V entry, the 4.8 V buck, the familiars, the pendulum and the theremin will work on the first boards. Levitation is not in that set: the published loop result does not include the driver that is on the board, so treat it as the experiment the plan already calls it (the same bat still swings). Do not hand the printed bat cap to a six-year-old as drawn; that fix is the print, not the copper, so it need not slip the panel order. Before paying JLCPCB, either leave the tree's two right-angle headers off the assembly (solder them after the tree is snapped out) or get a written yes for pins that hang below the panel over the rail. I would not hold the order for a copper respin of the base electronics.

## 2. Findings

### 1. Blocker for use — the bat cap is a loose hazardous magnet

- **Where:** [print/design.py](../print/design.py) (`CAP_FLOOR_T`, `CAP_HOOK_T`, `CAP_DOME_R`), [gen/mech.py](../gen/mech.py) (`MAGNET_D`, `MAGNET_T`, `POCKET_D`, `CAP_HOOK`, `BAT_HEAD_Y`), [print/check.py](../print/check.py) (roof check allows 0.8 mm over the cap magnet and 1.0 mm over the owl's).
- **What is wrong:** The play pieces are two 10 × 2 mm N52 discs. A disc that size is a hazardous magnet under ASTM F963 / 16 CFR 1250 if it can be swallowed or if it rides in a part that itself fits the small-parts cylinder. The bat cap is designed to snap off (0.8 mm hooks, strain checked so a grown-up can seat it), and the cap is small enough to fit the cylinder. The pause layer over a cold magnet is also the usual split plane; the check only requires 0.8 mm of plastic over the cap magnet and 0.4 mm under it.
- **Evidence:** Pole-face field of a 10 × 2 mm cylinder at Br = 1.43 T is B = (Br/2)·L/√(R²+L²) = 0.266 T = 2.66 kG. Area π·5² = 78.5 mm². Flux index ≈ 2.66² × 78.5 ≈ 550 kG²·mm², against a limit of 50. The small-parts cylinder is 31.75 mm in diameter, 25.4 mm deep at the wall and 57.1 mm on axis. The cap dome is about 13 mm across and 4.6 mm tall (`CAP_DOME_R` = pocket radius + 1.4 mm, `CAP_DOME_TOP` = 4.6 mm), and the hooks reach the head notch at y = −12.5 mm, so the cap is on the order of 25 mm long. At 6.6 mm off axis the cylinder is still about 44 mm deep, so a 25 × 13 × 6 mm cap fits. The bare bat PCB (47.8 × 25 mm) does **not** lie flat in the cylinder (depth at 12.5 mm off axis is only about 32 mm), so the board alone is probably not a small part; the removable cap is. Use-and-abuse for ages 3–8 is a 67 N pull, 133 N squeeze, 0.45 N·m torque, a soak and four 0.9 m drops. Nothing in `print/check.py` applies those loads. A 0.4 mm floor and a pause-print roof are not a demonstration that the magnet stays in.
- **Fix:** Before the child handles anything magnetic: make the magnet-bearing piece fail the small-parts cylinder in every orientation (a body or a tether a child cannot take off; two dimensions over 32 mm is not enough if the long axis still fits the deep part of the cylinder — check with a real template), and close the pocket so it survives the forces above. Do not rely on a snap-off cap as the only retention. The owl, which wraps the 34 mm perch and so does not fit the cylinder, still needs a roof and a pause-layer bond that survive a 67 N pull; 1.0 mm is the current minimum and is not that test. Spare magnets stay with a grown-up, as the print README already says. No PCB change.
- **Sure:** High on the flux index, the cap size, and the snap-off. Medium on whether a particular pause print would actually split; I did not break a sample.

### 2. Major — the tree headers hang below the panel, over the rail and the perch

- **Where:** [gen/placement.py](../gen/placement.py) (`J101` / `J102` on the bottom, `HEADER_BODY_FACE = 6.58`), [gen/layout.py](../gen/layout.py) (`PANEL_ROT["tree"] = 90`, `RAIL = 5`, `GAP = 2.4`, tree origin at `RAIL+GAP`), [gen/panel.py](../gen/panel.py) (clean edges at the header pins). The review package already states the pins reach 6 mm past the tree's top and bottom edges, below the panel plane.
- **What is wrong:** After the 90° turn, one header points at the left rail and one at the perch. The tree's left edge is 7.4 mm from the panel edge. Pins 6 mm past that edge reach about 1.4 mm from the panel outline and cross the 5 mm rail, on the bottom side of the panel. JLCPCB's line holds an assembled panel by the rails and the outline. Pins in free space below the panel, over the rail, are the usual reason a panel is held or the pins are bent in the conveyor. I cannot swear they will reject it; I would not pay until that is settled.
- **Evidence:** The tree is built unturned and then rotated 90° about its origin ([gen/board.py](../gen/board.py)). That origin sits at panel x = 7.4 mm (`RAIL+GAP`). The 112 mm header-to-header dimension then runs in x, so the right edge is at 119.4 mm and the perch's near edge is one `GAP` (2.4 mm) further, at 121.8 mm. Pins 6 mm past the right edge therefore overlap the perch by about 3.6 mm. Pins 6 mm past the left edge reach panel x ≈ 1.4 mm, across the 5 mm rail, still inside the outline. Both rows are on the bottom. I did not ask JLCPCB, and I did not re-measure the pin length from the stock `PinHeader_2x08_P2.54mm_Horizontal` model; the 6 mm is the package's own figure, and the placement comment already says the pins leave the board.
- **Fix, no copper change:** Mark J101 and J102 "do not place." The holes are already in the tree. Solder the two right-angle headers after the tree is snapped out; that is two parts and a few minutes, and it is the only through-hole that has to be done at home. If JLCPCB must solder them, add a sacrificial ear of FR4 under each pin row, inside the panel outline and clear of the perch, and snap it off with the mouse bites. Do not ship the panel as drawn and hope.
- **Sure:** High on the geometry. Medium on the factory's exact rule.

### 3. Major — the levitation "it holds" result is not this board's actuator

- **Where:** [sim/physics/levitation_loop.py](../sim/physics/levitation_loop.py) and [sim/physics/results/levitation_loop.json](../sim/physics/results/levitation_loop.json); [firmware/src/lev_ctrl.c](../firmware/src/lev_ctrl.c); [firmware/src/lev_table.h](../firmware/src/lev_table.h); [firmware/src/coils.c](../firmware/src/coils.c); [firmware/src/config.h](../firmware/src/config.h) (`LEV_PWM_HZ` 40 kHz, `LEV_I_MAX_A` 3.0). DRV8874 datasheet, fixed off-time 25 µs, blanking 1.1 µs. DRV5055A4 datasheet: 7.5 mV/mT (7.1–7.9) at 3.3 V, linear range ±169 mT.
- **What is wrong:** The JSON that says the loop holds (`peak_I_A` 0.81, jitter under a micrometre) is the old plant: 2.7 g, a 4 × 1 mm bias magnet, 31.5 µH / 3.35 Ω, an ideal ±12 V source, and a 25 mV/mT sensor. The firmware tables are the new geometry (10 × 2 mm, bias 14 mm up, 3.60 g, float gap 8.54 mm, coil grip 20.4 mN/A, Hall 8.66 mT/mm, coil field at the sensor 3.11 mT/A). Those tables are the right ones to have. The closed-loop proof was not re-run on them, and it never included the DRV8874.
- **Evidence:**
  - Coil as drawn: 30.1 µH, 2.56 Ω (`config.h`). L/R = 12 µs. At 12 V, di/dt = 0.40 A/µs, so 0 to 3.3 A takes 8.3 µs.
  - The bridge is phase-correct PWM at 40 kHz, period 25 µs, coast when both inputs are low (`coil_coast`). Average current wants duty = I·R/V. At 2.0 A that on-time is 11 µs, already longer than the 8.3 µs ramp to ITRIP, so a catch at the firmware's own "2.4 A" figure will hit the 3.3 A trip during the pulse.
  - IMODE is grounded: fixed off-time. tOFF is 25 µs, the same as the PWM period. The datasheet also says an input edge aborts the rest of tOFF. So the hardware limit is real, but it is not the clean voltage source the simulation integrates, and it fights a 40 kHz sign-magnitude PWM exactly when the catch asks for amperes.
  - The Hall sensor is in the hole. 3.11 mT/A is 0.36 mm of apparent gap per ampere if the subtraction is wrong. The firmware does subtract `kcs * i_now`, blending the model and IPROPI, which is the right idea, but IPROPI is low-side current, filtered (1 kΩ + 10 nF) and sampled at 10 kHz, while the coil field at the die follows the PWM. That residual is not in the 0.4 µm jitter number.
  - DRV5055A4 at 3.3 V is linear to ±169 mT. The bat table starts at 232 mT (1.5 mm gap) and is still about 167 mT at 2.9 mm, plus 6.4 mT of bias. Below about 3 mm the sensor is clipped. `gap_of_field` only reports "against the perch" if the reading reaches 232 mT, which this sensor cannot produce. A bat stuck to the perch looks like a gap of about 3 mm. The mean-current trip (0.6 A for a second) is the backstop, not the Hall test.
  - At the float point itself the numbers are comfortable: bat field about 34 mT, bias 6.4 mT, well inside ±169 mT, and 8.7 mT/mm is about 65 mV/mm into a 12-bit ADC. Small errors around 8.5 mm are sensible. The hole is the catch and the clip, not the float.
- **Fix:** Order anyway, and do not promise the float for Halloween. The pendulum uses the same bat. After the boards exist, the useful experiments are a current step into the perch coil with the Hall recorded (does `kcs` match 3.11 mT/A, and how much 40 kHz is left after the sensor), and a catch from 1 mm, not from 3 mm, until that trace is clean. No copper change is required to try. If the chopping is the thing that loses the bat, a 20 kΩ IMODE resistor (cycle-by-cycle) is a one-part respin later, not a reason to redraw rev A tonight.
- **Sure:** High that the published loop result is the wrong plant and omits the driver. Medium that the float will fail; a gentle catch may still work. Low that I can predict the aliasing without a scope.

### 4. Minor — 4.8 V versus the amplifier's 5.25 V absolute maximum, in the tolerance corner only

- **Where:** [gen/sch_base.py](../gen/sch_base.py) `buck()` (36 kΩ / 5.1 kΩ, two `C22U`); [gen/parts.py](../gen/parts.py) `C22U` is 22 µF 25 V, not the "two 10 µF" in the review package. NS4150B absolute maximum 5.25 V (datasheet front matter; the recommended operating table is written at 4.8 V). TPS54302: Vref 0.596 V typical, internal OVP when FB exceeds 108% of Vref, soft-start 5 ms.
- **What is wrong:** Steady state is fine: 0.596 × (1 + 36/5.1) = 4.80 V. A high corner (Vref +2%, both resistors 1% the wrong way) is about 4.99 V, still under 5.25 V. The buck's own OVP lets the rail reach 108% of that corner, about 5.39 V, for as long as the comparator takes to turn the high side off. That is above the amplifier's absolute maximum. It is a transient in a stacked tolerance, not the voltage the rail sits at.
- **Evidence:** arithmetic above, in [calculations.py](calculations.py). I did not extract the TPS54302 Vref min/max from the electrical table, so the +2% is a stand-in for a typical TI reference window, not a quoted limit.
- **Fix:** None before the order. On the first board, scope +5V at the amplifier from plug-in through a full LED load and a perch burst. If the peak is over 5.1 V, a later rev can add a clamp. Do not "fix" it by raising the setpoint.
- **Sure:** Medium. The 108% mechanism is in the datasheet; the corner-case peak is an estimate.

### 5. Nit — a few comments describe an older circuit

- **Where:** [DESIGN_REVIEW.md](../DESIGN_REVIEW.md) §10.2 says two 10 µF outputs; the generator fits two 22 µF. [firmware/src/board_pins.h](../firmware/src/board_pins.h) calls the volume antenna a pumpkin; the copper is the ghost ([gen/sch_base.py](../gen/sch_base.py) `osc(..., "VOL", "ghost")`). [firmware/src/familiar.h](../firmware/src/familiar.h) still quotes 28.5 µH and 4.2 Ω; the perch coils are 30.3 µH and 2.13 Ω.
- **What is wrong:** Nothing on the board. The familiar baseline is learned from empty sweeps, so the stale R and L do not set the detect threshold.
- **Fix:** Correct the comments when the package is next touched. Not an order hold.
- **Sure:** High.

## 3. Section 10

**1. Power entry.** The AO4407A stage is the right way round. Datasheet pinout matches the symbol: pins 1–3 source, 4 gate, 5–8 drain. The fuse feeds the drain, the source is the +12V pour, the gate is pulled to ground by 100 kΩ, and the BZT52C10 has its cathode (symbol pin 1) on the source and its anode (pin 2) on the gate. Vgs max is ±25 V; at 12 V the zener holds the gate about 10 V below the source, and it is what saves the gate if the rail rings. Body diode conducts drain-to-source until the channel enhances, so a reversed plug is blocked and a correct plug charges C1 immediately.

A 5 A PTC plus an SMBJ15A on the load side of the MOSFET is enough for a 12 V regulated brick and a long cord, with one caveat. The TVS is unidirectional, cathode to +12V, anode to ground, so it clamps a positive ring. The 470 µF polymer is the damper the note claims. The PTC part in the datasheet list really is the 16 V, 5 A hold device; 12 V is inside that voltage rating when the PTC is the thing that has opened. A 19 V laptop brick in the same 5.5 × 2.1 mm jack is not: the TVS will conduct continuously and the PTC is then over its 16 V rating. Label the jack and the brick. I would not add a crowbar for a one-off whose adapter is specified.

The 12 V copper is enough for the pulses this firmware actually commands, not for four bridges sitting at ITRIP. IPC-2221, external 1 oz, 10 °C: 1.0 mm carries about 2.4 A continuous, 0.4 mm about 1.2 A. Pendulum firmware limit is 0.5 A. A levitation catch on one bridge can ask for 2–3 A for a short time; a few tens of milliseconds in a short 0.4 mm neck at the HTSSOP pin is a couple of degrees, not a fuse. All four bridges at 3.3 A would be about 13 A, which the 1 mm trunk cannot carry and which the PTC will eventually open. That case is a firmware fault, and nSLEEP has a 4.7 kΩ to ground so a reset puts the bridges to sleep. I do not see a power-entry change that should delay the order.

**2. The 4.8 V buck.** The generator is in better shape than the question text: two 22 µF 25 V ceramics, which is TI's own example for this part, plus 10 µF and 100 nF on the input and 100 nF boot. 10 µH at 400 kHz gives about 0.7 A of ripple at 12 V to 4.8 V, fine for a 4.5 A-saturation inductor at a 1–1.5 A load. EN through 100 kΩ / 22 kΩ is about 2.2 V at 12 V and crosses a 1.2 V threshold near 6.7 V, as the note says; the internal pull-up is microamps and does not move that much. The UCC27524's recommended minimum is 4.5 V and its UVLO is typically 4.2 V, so 4.8 V has a little room but not a lot; a sag under the LED step is the thing to scope, not a reason to redraw. Planned copper is the right shape (VIN to the input capacitors, SW short then wide to the inductor, output pour into the plane through three vias). I did not render it. See finding 4 for the amplifier absolute-maximum corner. I would order this buck.

**3. Levitation.** The float point in the new tables is believable: 8.54 mm, 5.0 N/m unstable, 20 mN/A, Hall well inside the A4's range, coil field at the sensor only 3.1 mT/A so a quiet loop sees mostly the bat. The loop margin in `levitation_loop.json` is not the margin of this hardware. See finding 3. I would not bet Halloween on the float. I would still order the coil, the Hall sensor and the bridge, because the pendulum needs the bat and a failed float costs a firmware evening, not a respin.

**4. Familiars.** The detect path is the right kind of detector. A sweep at two small duties cancels the driver's own draw, a fit against a learned empty-perch baseline asks for a resonance, and a poor fit (a coin, a key) is not driven ([firmware/src/familiar.h](../firmware/src/familiar.h)). A plain key or a phone's metal back should look like extra loss, not a tuned peak. A phone with a Qi coil is itself a tank near 100–200 kHz and could fit as "a token"; the drive is then capped near 0.2 A and 0.25 W, which is unlikely to hurt the phone and will not look like the owl. I would not add hardware to reject that.

LED current is bounded by the 4.8 V bridge, not by hope. Empty-perch reactance of 30.3 µH is 27 Ω at 142 kHz and 58 Ω at 303 kHz, so a 4.8 V square wave (fundamental 4/π × 4.8 = 6.1 V) cannot push more than about 0.23 A and 0.11 A. The firmware also refuses a duty that would put more than 0.92 × 0.2 A into an empty perch, and it drives at the token's own tuning, where the token raises the impedance. 150 Ω in each LED leg is extra margin on top of that, not the only margin. The schematic note's "about 20 mA" is the right order; I did not re-integrate `familiar.py` (it writes into `sim/physics/results/`). I do not see a token-LED change for rev A.

**5. Theremin antennas.** I did not re-run the antenna solver. The oscillator is a 74LVC1G14, 100 kΩ, 15 pF plus the pour, counted for 10 ms. Temperature drift of a Schmitt gate is real, but both thresholds move, and the firmware does not trust a factory zero: it calibrates on start and, after 5 s with nobody playing, walks the baseline with a 10 s time constant ([firmware/src/theremin.c](../firmware/src/theremin.c)), and it will not learn away a hand that is already a quarter of the span. Humidity on a bare ENIG pour can still move C by a fraction of a picofarad, which is the same size as a distant hand. That makes the far end of the gesture soft, not dead. For a toy, with the baseline tracker, I would ship it. The "3%" in the question is an assumption in `theremin.h`, not a measurement I repeated.

**6. GPIO binding.** The constraints match the RP2350 function table, and the generated header obeys them. Each bridge is one PWM slice, A on the even GPIO and B on the odd, all on GPIO0–15. Audio is PWM4 A (GPIO8); GPIO9 is the volume PIO input and is not claimed as PWM. HALL, LEV_IPROPI and MUX_OUT are GPIO27–29, the ADC pins. I2C0 is GPIO20/21 and UART0 is GPIO16/17, both legal for those instances. The mux selects are GPIO24–26, consecutive. GPIO26 is an ADC-capable pin used as a digital select, which is allowed. The 84 crossings are mostly the back-edge pins (GPIO19–29 face the USB side) having to reach the front. I did not exhaust the legal permutations for a lower crossing count. A lower count would not change a single function, and the board already routes with the crossings it has. I would not rebind for rev A.

**7. The panel.** Fiducials, tooling holes and the 5 mm rails match the usual JLCPCB customer-panel list, and the mouse bites are kept off courtyards and off the jack, USB, switch and notches. The thing I would not sign is the header overhang in finding 2. The rest of the panel (245 × 223 mm, inside 250 × 250 mm, 23 tabs) is a normal assembled panel. Order it once the headers are DNP or accepted.

**8. CPL rotations.** I read the method (180° minus the angle, X not negated, checked against JLCPCB's drawings of the chip parts) and I did not redraw their rotation figures myself, and I have not seen their assembly preview. Chip parts with a clear pin-1 are the ones that method can get right. LEDs and diodes, which the package already flags as "predicted, not seen in the preview," are the ones that silently reverse. A reversed SMBJ15A is a short on +12V; a reversed token LED is a dark familiar. Open the preview and look at D1, D2, D3, D4, the LEDs and the tantalum before paying. I am not reporting a rotation I proved wrong.

**9. The RP2354A corner.** The circuit is Raspberry Pi's: 27 Ω on D+/D−, 5.1 kΩ on CC1 and CC2, ABM8-272-T3 with 15 pF for a 10 pF crystal and 1 kΩ on XOUT, 100 nF on every supply pin, 4.7 µF on the 1.1 V regulator. Moving a decoupling capacitor 0.5–2 mm at 12 MHz, 1 MHz I2C and PWM is not a functional change; the review package's own judgement there is right. I did not render the crop, so I have not seen whether XIN/XOUT stayed short and over the ground pour. Given that those two nets are planned copper rather than maze-routed, I expect them to be fine, and I would not hold the order to redraw them. First-article check is that the crystal starts and USB enumerates, which this chip does with this exact network on other boards.

**10. Child safety.** Finding 1 is the one that matters. Beyond it: 12 V from a listed brick is outside the mains-toy rules (the toy side is under 30 V), the PTC is the short-circuit fuse, and the sound switch holds the amplifier in shutdown even if firmware goes wrong — that interlock is the right one, and the default volume is 0.12 of a scale the note puts near 0.5 W. Violet is firmware-capped at 0.6 of the LP5036 channel (`LED_CEIL_VIOLET`); if a child stares at a star from a few centimetres, lower that constant, no respin. Mouse-bite edges will be sharp after the snap; sand them before the board is a toy. The coil 45 °C limit is a resistance model, not a sensor beside the copper. It is the right idea and it is unproven until the first board is warm; do not skip that check, and do not let a hand be the thermometer.

## 4. What I checked and found right

- Reverse-polarity MOSFET orientation, zener polarity, TVS polarity, and the body-diode path into C1 and the TVS. Gate clamp is inside the ±25 V rating.
- Buck divider arithmetic, EN divider, boot capacitor, and the fact that the fitted output capacitors are TI's 2 × 22 µF example rather than the stale 10 µF in the question.
- USB-C as a device: 5.1 kΩ on both CC pins, 27 Ω on D+ and D−, VBUS not back-fed onto the LED rail. The two Schottkys OR into VSYS only. XC6206 is the ceramic-stable LDO, not an AMS1117.
- DRV8874 straps: PMODE high, IMODE to ground, nSLEEP held down by 4.7 kΩ through reset, nFAULT pulled up. ITRIP arithmetic, 3.3 V / (2.2 kΩ × 450 µA/A) = 3.3 A, matches the datasheet formula. Charge-pump capacitors are present.
- UCC27524 enables pulled down, so a reset cannot leave a perch coil driven. Duty is clamped so the two legs are never high together.
- Familiar addressing: owl 100 nF / 12.5 µH at 142 kHz, cat 22 nF at 303 kHz, 150 Ω per LED, drive from 4.8 V not 12 V. The current cap above is why a firmware bug cannot cook the token LEDs.
- Hall part versus firmware: 7.5 mV/mT at 3.3 V is the datasheet typical (7.1–7.9), not the 12.5 mV/mT 5 V number. Quiescent VCC/2 matches. The float-point field is inside the linear range.
- PWM slice pairing, ADC pin choice, I2C0 and UART0 instances, consecutive mux selects. Reset pull-downs on nSLEEP and FAM_EN are in the header as well as the schematic.
- Sound switch: 100 kΩ holds CTRL low, and +5V is absent without the adapter, so USB-only is silent even if the switch is on.
- I2C 2.2 kΩ pull-ups at 400 kHz. Rail dividers: 12 V through 100 kΩ/22 kΩ is 2.16 V, inside the 3.3 V mux; a ring to 24 V injects through 100 kΩ, not through a wire.
- Levitation tables match the one-magnet geometry the print uses (14 mm lift, 8.54 mm gap, 3.60 g), even though the old loop JSON does not.
- Panel size, rail, fiducial inset and mouse-bite diameter are in the range JLCPCB publishes for a customer panel. DRC-clean and schematic parity are claimed by the package; I did not re-run DRC, because that writes into the tree the other reviewers are reading.

## 5. What I could not check

- A rebuild, a DRC rerun, or a copper render. Those write into `hardware/`, `build/` and `firmware/src/`, which I was told not to touch. So I have not seen the buck loop or the crystal traces with my own eyes; I read the planner.
- JLCPCB's assembly preview and a written answer on the overhanging headers.
- A re-run of `levitation_loop.py` or `familiar.py` on the as-built coils. Both write JSON into `sim/physics/results/`.
- The host firmware tests. `make -C firmware/test` would write objects outside this directory. I read the controller instead.
- Humidity coefficient of the ENIG antennas, and the actual radiant power of the 405 nm LED at the firmware ceiling. Both can be lowered in firmware if the first board is brighter or drifter than expected.
- A small-parts template and a pull test on a real print. The geometry says the cap fits; a cylinder is the confirmation.
- Datasheet figures I would otherwise have pasted. They are copyrighted and stay in `datasheets/`.
